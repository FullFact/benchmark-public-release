Data dimensions

The original five dimensions are labelled * and are based on human annotation, as is the URL quality score. The others are predicted by a Gemma model's post hoc analysis of the first full sentence of each response.

Note that some dimensions only make sense for certain questions. For example, polarity is only defined for questions with a clear yes/no answer, but will still have a value for other questions.

Question used in examples below: How many cases were there?
Factuality*
Proportion of "essential" paragraphs in the marking scheme found in the response.

Bands: the factuality dimension can be expanded to show the proportion of answers that are completely correct (containing atoms linked to all essential paragraphs); mostly correct (atoms match 50-99% of essential paragraphs); partly correct (1-49%) and incorrect (0% of essential paragraphs have at least one associated atom).

Certainty
How certain is the chatbot that the claim is true?
High: It was about 1000 cases.
Low: It might have been about 1000 cases.
Precision
How exactly is the claim stated?
High: It was 1000 cases.
Low: It was about 1000 cases.
Directness
Is the chatbot answering the question directly, or relying on an external source?
Direct (1): It was 1000 cases.
Indirect (0): According to the WHO, it was 1000 cases.
Polarity
Is the answer 'yes' or 'no'?
Yes (1): It was 1000 cases.
No (-1): It was not 1000 cases.

The polarity view can be expanded to reveal the bipolar and non-polar dimensions, defined here:

Bipolar
Is the answer both 'yes' and 'no'?
Bipolar (1): It was 1000 cases but it later fell.
Not bipolar (0): It was 1000 cases.
Non-Polar
Neither agrees with nor disagrees with the question.
Non-polar (1): It was 1000 cases but then it wasn't.
Consistency*
What proportion of the atoms are the same over time in response to this question?
URL quality
Mean quality rating of the URLs cited in the response.
Primary 3, good 2, OK 1, poor −1.
Falsehoods
How many of the response's claims contain atoms that are false or misleading?
False or misleading: There were no cases.
Irrelevant and false: The moon is made of cheese.
Info location
How far into the response the last piece of essential information appears, as a fraction of the response's length. 0 means the essential information is all at the very start; 1 means the reader had to read to the end to get it all.
0.1: There were 1000 cases. The outbreak began in May and …
0.9: The outbreak began in May and … so there were 1000 cases.

Words: the info location dimension can be expanded to show the same figure as a raw word count rather than a fraction. Responses in which no essential information was found are not counted in either.

Civic responsibility*
For questions tagged "civics", how factual is the response?
Timeliness*
If the truth-state changed over time, did the response contain the updated information?
Timely (1): Andy Burnham became PM in June 2026.
Not timely (0): Keir Starmer is still PM in October 2026.
Transparency*
Does the response acknowledge complexity, multiple points of view etc.?
Not shown here for some reason.
