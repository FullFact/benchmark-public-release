Data dimensions

The original five dimensions are labelled * and are based on human annotation, as is the URL quality score. The others are predicted by a Gemma model's post hoc analysis of the first full sentence of each response.

Question used in examples below: How many cases were there?
Factuality*
Proportion of "essential" paragraphs in the marking scheme found in the response.
Certainty
How certain is the chatbot that the claim is true?
High: It was about 1000 cases.
Low: It might have been about 1000 cases.
Precision
How exactly is the claim stated?
High: It was 1000 cases.
Low: It was about 1000 cases.
Directness
Is the chatbot answering the question directly, or relying on an external source?
Direct (1): It was 1000 cases.
Indirect (0): According to the WHO, it was 1000 cases.
Polarity
Is the answer 'yes' or 'no'?
Yes (1): It was 1000 cases.
No (-1): It was not 1000 cases.
Bipolar
Is the answer both 'yes' and 'no'?
Bipolar (1): It was 1000 cases but it later fell.
Not bipolar (0): It was 1000 cases.
Non Polar
Neither agrees with nor disagrees with the question.
Consistency*
What proportion of the atoms are the same over time in response to this question?
URL quality
Mean quality rating of the URLs cited in the response.
Primary 3, good 2, OK 1, poor −1.
Civic responsibility*
For questions tagged "civics", how factual is the response?
Timeliness*
If the truth-state changed over time, did the response contain the updated information?
Timely (1): Andy Burnham became PM in June 2026.
Not timely (0): Keir Starmer is still PM in October 2026.
Transparency*
Does the response acknowledge complexity, multiple points of view etc.?
Not shown here for some reason.
